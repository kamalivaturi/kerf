In short

  • This website has no analytics, advertising, tracking pixels, accounts, forms or comment boxes. It sets no cookies.
  • It is hosted by GitHub Pages, and GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses. The project does not collect those logs.
  • Your browser asks GitHub for the latest release so the page can show the current version and the right download.
  • If you press the theme button, your light or dark choice is saved in your own browser and goes nowhere else.
  • The Kerf software runs on your computer. It has no Kerf account, no usage tracking and no Kerf server. Some optional features contact services you choose; section 4 lists every one.

4. The Kerf software

Kerf’s engine runs on your own computer and listens only on 127.0.0.1, so other computers cannot reach it. Every request needs a token kept in your user folder. Your footage, projects, transcripts and generated files stay on your computer unless you use one of the features in the table below.

What Kerf keeps on your computer

  • A .kerf folder in your user folder: settings, the access token, caches, downloaded models, presets, your custom vocabulary, and a log of the last 50 engine errors with your home folder shortened to ~.
  • API keys you enter for AI providers are stored in settings.json in that folder as plain text. The panel shows them masked and sends each key only to the provider it belongs to. Protect your user account accordingly.
  • The panels keep layout preferences in the editor’s local storage on your device.
  • Kerf Studio, the background helper, starts when you log in. It checks the list of running programs on your computer to see whether Premiere Pro or After Effects is open, and it stops soon after they close. That check stays on your computer.

Kerf’s own code contains no analytics, crash reporting, advertising identifier or usage tracking, and Kerf has no server of its own to send data to. We checked this for version 0.3.2. Libraries Kerf uses, such as the AI provider kits and the model downloader, add their usual client details (library version, operating system) to their own requests. Each provider applies its own privacy policy, so read it before you send it transcripts or audio.

Kerf’s code also supports optional licence keys. No feature in the free version needs one. If you paste a key into Settings anyway, Kerf sends the key and your computer’s name to the licence service, Lemon Squeezy, to activate it, and checks again about once a week. This page will be updated if that changes.

5. Sharing, keeping and your rights

We do not sell or share personal information, we do not profile anyone, and nothing here makes automated decisions about you. This site and Kerf do not hold personal data about you that we could look up. If you have written to us, we hold what you sent, and we keep it only as long as needed to answer you.

  • EEA and UK (GDPR, UK GDPR): you may ask for access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, and object to processing, and you may complain to your data protection authority, such as the ICO in the UK.
  • India (Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023): you may ask for a summary of data held, ask for correction or erasure, use our grievance contact above, and nominate someone to exercise your rights. If we do not resolve a complaint, you may approach the Data Protection Board of India once it is operating.
  • California (CCPA/CPRA): you may ask what we hold and to delete or correct it, and you will not be treated differently for asking. We do not sell or share personal information.

To make a request about something you sent us, write to the contact above. For the server logs, contact GitHub. Our reply time is 30 days.

6. Security

The site is served over HTTPS by GitHub. Updates are downloaded only after their signature is checked. The engine accepts connections only from your own computer and only with its token. No system is perfectly secure; report a vulnerability through the project’s GitHub issues without including secrets, or by email.

7. Laws we considered

This is our reading, not legal advice.

  • GDPR and UK GDPR apply to anyone offering a site to people in the EEA or UK. The only personal data involved here is the IP address handled by GitHub, described above.
  • India: the DPDP Act 2023 and the DPDP Rules 2025 (notified 13 November 2025, with most duties applying from May 2027), the Information Technology Act 2000 (section 43A) and the 2011 Sensitive Personal Data Rules. Kerf collects none of the sensitive personal data those rules list. We still follow their transparency, security and grievance ideas.
  • CCPA/CPRA: as far as we know, this project is below the thresholds that make a business subject to it, and it does not sell or share personal information anyway.

8. Children

This site and Kerf are made for video editors and are not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect children’s data. Under the DPDP Act a child is anyone under 18.
